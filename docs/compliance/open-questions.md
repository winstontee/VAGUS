## Q1. Can she counsel clients online in other provinces? (top priority)

**Short answer.** It depends on where the **client is physically located
during the session**, not where Amber is. Ontario and Quebec are the two
places she should not take counselling clients now. Nova Scotia, New
Brunswick and PEI mainly restrict titles, but each has conditions. Until
this is confirmed, the site should not say "across Canada".

### Does the client's location decide it? Yes, in practice. Confidence: Medium-High.
- CRPO frames its rules around "clients **in Ontario**" (O1, O2).
- NSCCT's policy covers "clients **located in** Nova Scotia" (N1).
- OPQ says jurisdictions "generally require a licence for remote services to a
  person **on their territory**" (Q3).
- The PEI telepractice limits count PEI clients (N5).
- Practical consequence: a BC client who joins a session while travelling in
  Ontario is, for that session, a client in Ontario. Intake should confirm the
  client's location at the start of each session.

### Ontario (CRPO, controlled act of psychotherapy). Confidence: Medium-High that this is a real risk.
- The controlled act is treating a **serious disorder of** thought,
  cognition, mood, **emotional regulation**, perception or **memory** that may
  seriously impair functioning. It has to be done by psychotherapy technique
  within a therapeutic relationship (O5).
- Trauma counselling for people with C-PTSD patterns is the core of Amber's
  work. It is relational, and it addresses emotional dysregulation and trauma
  memory. That could plausibly meet all the elements. This is our reading, not
  a CRPO ruling.
- Non-registrants "cannot perform the Controlled Act on clients in Ontario"
  (O2). CRPO says it **may take action** against unregulated out-of-province
  providers who do (O1).
- CRPO's leniency toward "infrequent e-therapy" applies to practitioners who
  are **regulated** elsewhere (O1). Amber isn't: BC doesn't regulate
  counselling yet, and ACCT is a voluntary body.
- ACCT itself gives Ontario residents a different title ("ACc", not "RTCc")
  (A4, A7). That suggests ACCT accepts Ontario's title rules matter.
- Title: don't use "psychotherapist" or "psychotherapy" in any way that
  targets Ontario.
- **Recommendation:** don't offer 1:1 counselling to clients located in
  Ontario. Education (the program) is a different question; see Q4.

### Quebec (OPQ, psychotherapy permit). Confidence: Medium.
- Under Code des professions art. 187.1, psychotherapy is reserved to
  psychologists, physicians and holders of an OPQ psychotherapist permit. The
  title "psychothérapeute" is also reserved (Q2, Q6).
- OPQ's out-of-province leniency is written for **psychologists** who are
  "legally authorised to practise in their home jurisdiction" (Q3). We found
  nothing that extends it to an unregulated counsellor. CCPA says OPQ
  publishes no interprovincial guidance for psychotherapy (Q5).
- Quebec's list of things that are **not** psychotherapy includes support
  interventions, psychoeducation and coaching (Q1). It's possible some of
  Amber's 1:1 work falls there. But trauma-focused relational counselling for
  people in distress is close to the core of what the law reserves.
- **Recommendation:** don't offer 1:1 counselling to clients located in
  Quebec.

### Nova Scotia (NSCCT, title protection). Confidence: Medium-High on titles, Medium on practice.
- Providers from non-regulated provinces (BC is one) may **not** use
  "Counselling Therapist", "Registered Counselling Therapist" or "RCT" with
  Nova Scotians. They may not advertise their work as "counselling therapy"
  either (N1).
- Complaints about such providers go **exclusively to NSCCT** (N1). So NSCCT
  claims oversight of conduct toward NS clients.
- ACCT gives NS residents "ACc", not "RTCc" (A4). Whether ACCT expects BC
  members to change their title when serving NS clients is unknown.
- Watch out: ACCT's own name contains "Counselling Therapists". Naming the
  association is probably fine. Calling herself a "counselling therapist" is
  not.
- **Recommendation:** possible, but only after ACCT and her insurer confirm.
  Use "counsellor" only, never "counselling therapy" or "therapist". Tell the
  client she isn't registered with NSCCT.

### New Brunswick. Confidence: Low-Medium.
- NB protects "Licensed Counselling Therapist", in force 2017 (N3).
  Secondary sources say out-of-province counsellors aren't barred from serving
  NB clients "as long as this is permitted by their own regulator" (N3). BC
  has no regulator for her, so that condition is unclear.
- **Recommendation:** possible after ACCT and insurer confirm. Use no
  protected titles and disclose her status.

### Prince Edward Island. Confidence: Low-Medium.
- The regulation is "title protection only". Protected titles are "counselling
  therapist" and "CT" (N4).
- A secondary summary of the CCTPEI telepractice policy (N5) says that without
  registration she may **not advertise in PEI**, may see **at most 3 PEI
  clients at a time**, and PEI clients must be **under 20% of her caseload**.
  She must also tell clients how to complain. The CCTPEI original was not seen.
- **Recommendation:** possible within those limits. Get the CCTPEI
  telepractice policy itself before taking any PEI client.

### Other provinces and territories (not researched in depth)
- Alberta is moving counselling therapists under the College of Alberta
  Psychologists. No in-force date found (N7). Newfoundland and Labrador, SK,
  MB and the territories appear unregulated for counselling (N6, Q5). That
  could change, so recheck before adding any of them to the site.

- Intake form: ask "Which province or territory will you be in for our

## Q2. BC regulation status and title restrictions

**Findings.** Confidence: Medium-High.
- The **HPOA came into force on 1 April 2026**, replacing the Health
  Professions Act (B5).
- **Psychotherapy** was designated in July 2024. Under B.C. Reg. 131/2025 it
  becomes regulated by **CHCPBC on 29 November 2027** (B1, B3, B4).
- **Protected title:** "psychotherapist". The Ministry's Q&A (updated
  27 Aug 2026) reportedly says unlicensed practitioners **may keep using
  unprotected titles such as "counsellor" and "counselling therapist"** (B1).
  A 2024 news report suggested counsellor-type titles might be covered (B7),
  but the newer Ministry Q&A supersedes it. Confirm against the full Q&A.
- **Restricted activity:** the Ministry consulted from 7 July to 5 August 2026
  on a new restricted activity. It would cover only the "highest-risk
  psychotherapeutic care interventions" for significant disorders with
  impairment (B2). **The final wording isn't published.** This is the piece
  that matters most for Amber. If intensive trauma work for C-PTSD is
  captured, an unlicensed RTCc may not be able to do it after November 2027,
  whatever her title.
- Eligibility, education criteria, any grandparenting route and fees are
  **not yet set**. CHCPBC is expected to consult on eligibility in fall 2026
  (B8; secondary).
- No ACCT statement on how RTCs map onto CHCPBC licensing was found.

**What this means now.**
- Never use "psychotherapist" or "psychotherapy" about her own work. This is
  already in CLAUDE.md's spirit, so add "psychotherapy" to the banned list.
- "Counsellor" and "counselling" look safe through 2027, based on the snippet.
- Before November 2027, decide whether to apply to CHCPBC. That depends on
  her education and whether there's a grandparenting route.

## Q3. ACCT's own rules: advertising, testimonials, candidate titles, supervision disclosure

**Access.** acctcounsellor.com failed DNS in this session. Earlier, it served

### Advertising. Confidence: Medium.
- §7.1: accurately represent competencies, education, training and
  experience, "**without reference to, or claims of, particular outcomes**"
  (A2). This directly supports CLAUDE.md's "no promises" rule. It also bears
  on wording like "new neural pathways"; see the site review.
- §7.2: ads must give the public what it needs to choose, and be "consistent
  with applicable law" (A2). That pulls provincial title laws (Q1) into ACCT
  compliance.
- §7.5: don't present specialised services without the education, training
  or supervised experience to back them (A2). This matters for "C-PTSD" and
  "EMDR" claims.
- The Scope of Practice refers to Standards §2.6.2 (Accurate Advertising) and
  §2.4.16 (Professional Misconduct) (A3). The numbering differs from §7, so
  there may be two versions. Use whichever is dated 22 Aug 2025.

### Testimonials. Confidence: Low-Medium.
- §7.6 is titled "Testimonials". The visible fragment says members "**do not
  solicit testimonials from clients**". The rest is cut off (A2).
- CLAUDE.md is already stricter: no testimonials from current clients at all.
  Keep that rule. Don't use former-client testimonials either until §7.6 has
  been read in full.

### Candidate title. Confidence: Medium-High.
- The ACCT Code and Standards apply to RTCcs (A1).
- The title is "RTCc" (Registered Therapeutic Counsellor Candidate). In
  **Ontario and Nova Scotia** it's "ACc" (A4).
- **RTCc status is capped at 2 years** (A4, A5). So the site's credentials
  need a review date.
- CLAUDE.md allows only "RTC Candidate (ACCT)" or "RTCc". ACCT's full form,
  "Registered Therapeutic Counsellor Candidate", contains the banned string
  "Registered Therapeutic Counsellor". Using the full form only together with
  "Candidate" is accurate. But it's a judgement call for the CLAUDE.md owner.
  Until they decide, use the two approved forms.

### Supervision disclosure. Confidence: Low on the marketing question, Medium-High that she's supervised.
- The RTCc "**must be under direct supervision**" (A4). Candidates enter with
  supervised hours (A6).
- We found **no ACCT text requiring candidates to name their supervisor in
  marketing**. Other bodies' norms, such as the ACA, ACES and CCE codes,
  require supervisees to tell **clients** about their supervisee status, and
  often the supervisor's name, in **informed consent**, not in advertising.
  Those codes don't bind her; they're context only.
- **What her site says now:** About, line 13 mentions "supervised clinical
  hours in progress", but ties them to the CPCA designation. It doesn't say
  that she practises under supervision as an RTCc.
- **Recommendation until confirmed:** say on the site that she practises
  under clinical supervision. Name the supervisor in the client consent form,

  regular clinical supervision."

## Q4. Program framing: what makes it "education" rather than counselling?

**Findings.** Confidence: Medium.
- **Ontario:** CRPO lists teaching, social skill development, **emotion
  regulation** and **prescriptive programs** as outside the controlled act,
  if that's all that's provided (O3). The risk returns if the program becomes
  individualised treatment of a serious disorder within a therapeutic
  relationship (O5).
- **Quebec:** "**éducation psychologique**" (learning through information
  and teaching) is listed as **not** psychotherapy (Q1, Q7). "Coaching" is
  also listed, but it's defined for people **not** in distress (Q7). So
  "coaching" is the wrong label for a C-PTSD audience in Quebec. Call it
  education.
- **BC:** currently unregulated. After November 2027, the restricted activity
  (Q2) only targets the highest-risk interventions. A skills course should sit
  outside it, but recheck once the final wording is out.
- **Competition Act s. 74.01(1)(b):** any performance or efficacy claim ("calms
  your nervous system", "reduces anxiety in 6 weeks") must rest on an
  **adequate and proper test done beforehand** (P1). Health-related claims
  get a stricter standard.
- **CASL** applies to the email list: consent, sender identification, and
  unsubscribe handled within 10 business days (P2; confirm with CRTC).

**What keeps it education. Checklist for program copy and design**
- [ ] Call it a **course, class or skills program**. Never "therapy",
      "treatment", "counselling", "healing program", "trauma recovery
      program" or "group therapy".
- [ ] Teach **general** nervous-system information and regulation and
      grounding skills. No individual assessment, no treatment plans, no
      one-on-one processing in group calls. No trauma memory work, which
      CLAUDE.md already bans.
- [ ] Live Q&A covers course content only. Rules for participants: don't share
      trauma details. The facilitator redirects personal disclosures and
      offers referral info.
- [ ] Same content for everyone. No individual tailoring based on symptoms.
- [ ] A screening note at signup: not suitable during acute crisis; how to
      find counselling or crisis help.
- [ ] Crisis info on the sales page, in every email, and in every module.
- [ ] No outcome promises or timelines. Describe what's taught, not what
      will change.
- [ ] Amber is listed as the course creator. Her RTCc can appear as
      background, but **don't** imply the course is delivered as an RTCc
      service.
- [ ] **Dual relationships:** decide whether current 1:1 clients may join,
      and how she'll avoid undue influence when promoting it to them. Check
      ACCT's dual-relationship standard.
- [ ] Terms of sale: refund policy, no-guarantee clause, privacy (PIPA BC),
      CASL-compliant signup.

**Draft disclaimer** (for her to rewrite in her own voice):
> This course is education about the nervous system, plus practical
> regulation and grounding skills. It isn't counselling, psychotherapy or
> medical treatment, and it doesn't replace care from a qualified
> professional. Joining doesn't make me your counsellor. If you're in crisis
> or thinking about suicide, call or text 9-8-8 any time. In an emergency,
> call 9-1-1.

## Q5. Crisis resources: official wording

**Findings.** Confidence: High for 9-8-8 and 9-1-1. Medium for the BC lines,
whose hours need confirming.
- **9-8-8 Suicide Crisis Helpline:** call or text **9-8-8**. Free, 24/7,

  For anyone thinking about suicide or worried about someone. Standard text
  rates may apply (C3).
