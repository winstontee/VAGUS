# Positioning: The Vagus Effect

Status: **draft v1, 2026-10-28.** Content and site can draft from this. It
still needs ethics review, and Amber should rewrite it in her own voice.
Owner: strategist.

Everything here builds on her current site language (see
`docs/research/current-site.md`): patterns that "developed for a reason",
curiosity, not judgement, "at a pace that feels right for you", working
*with* the nervous system, and no label or diagnosis needed. The aim is to
sharpen what she already says, not to replace it.

---

## 1. One-sentence niche statement

> **Online counselling for adults in British Columbia who have been bracing,
> people-pleasing or shutting down for as long as they can remember, working
> gently with the nervous system to understand patterns that developed for a
> reason, with no label needed to begin.**

Why it's built this way:

- **"Adults in British Columbia"**: for now, channels should target BC only
  (see "Geography" below). The site currently says "across Canada". Leave
  that to the ethics reviewer.
- **"Bracing, people-pleasing or shutting down"**: these are the reader's
  own experiences, taken from her Services page (on edge, overwhelmed,
  numbing, people-pleasing). They are not clinical terms. The reader can
  recognise herself without being diagnosed (CLAUDE.md: no diagnosing
  readers).
- **"For as long as they can remember"**: points at long-running or
  relational (complex) trauma without naming it. ICD-11 describes complex
  PTSD as following prolonged or repeated trauma that is hard to escape, with
  ongoing difficulties in emotion regulation, self-concept and relationships
  ([Brewin 2020, BJPsych](https://www.cambridge.org:443/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/E53B8CD7CF9B725FE651720EE58E93A4/S0007125020000434a.pdf/icd11_complex_posttraumatic_stress_disorder_simplifying_diagnosis_in_trauma_populations.pdf)).
  We describe the experience and keep the label for search pages.
- **"Developed for a reason" / "no label needed"**: her own words. They are
  the strongest thing she has.
- No outcome promise. The sentence says what she does, not what the client
  will get (CLAUDE.md: no promises of cure, recovery timelines or guaranteed
  outcomes).

Short version for bios and headers (about 10 words):

> **Nervous-system-informed counselling for long-held trauma patterns. No label needed.**

Credential line to pair with either version (never shorten it to "RTC" or
"therapist"):

> Amber Shanahan, RTC Candidate (ACCT). Online, based in Vancouver, BC.

## 3. Who it's not for (or not yet)

turn them away.

## 4. What makes her approach different

Ranked by how much each one matters to this audience. The ranking is a
judgement call, not data.

1. **No label needed, and nothing is "wrong" with you.** Her framing is that
   patterns developed for a reason. Many people with long-running trauma
   have heard that something is wrong with them their whole lives. Being
   non-pathologising is the main promise, not a footnote. (Her site already
   leads with this tone. Keep it.)
2. **The body and nervous system come first, at your pace.** Regulation and
   capacity come before story. For people who freeze or flood when they try
   to talk, this is the practical difference from talk-only counselling.
   *Credibility note:* polyvagal theory, which much "vagus" content draws
   on, is scientifically contested
   ([Grossman 2023, summarised here](https://journalofpsychiatryreform.com/2023/10/17/polyvagal-approaches-scientifically-questionable-but-useful-in-practice/);
   [Porges's reply via the Polyvagal Institute](https://www.polyvagalinstitute.org/criticaldiscussionofpolyvagaltheory)).
   Copy should describe *experiences* ("on edge", "shut down", "settling")
   and practical skills, not make firm anatomical claims about the vagus
   nerve. This protects her with a well-read, sceptical audience.
3. **Relational and attachment-aware.** The relationship is part of the
   work. That matters for people whose hurt happened in relationships.
4. **Whole-person background.** She is also a Registered Holistic
   Nutritionist and has somatic training (her About page). Sleep, food,
   energy and the body are all on the table. Content should say *how* she
   brings this in, without promising outcomes. [PLACEHOLDER: does she
   integrate nutrition into counselling sessions, or keep it separate?]
5. **Lived experience.** Her own path into the work (About page).
   `[HER STORY: only her words, only what she chooses to share]`
6. **Accessible candidate fee.** As an RTCc, a lower fee than RCCs is
   honest and is an advantage (see `offer-ladder.md`). It partly makes up
   for the lack of insurance coverage.

What isn't unique: "trauma-informed somatic counselling" in Vancouver is a
crowded phrase. *(Guess, based on how often it appears in the BC practice
sites that came up during rate research. Not a counted competitor scan.)*
The niche comes from the **combination** of her tone, the "no label"
promise, the body-first pace, a lower candidate fee and her background, not
from any one of these.

## 6. Geography

Her site says "online counselling across Canada". Until the ethics reviewer
answers the open compliance question, **all channels, directory listings,
keywords and copy should say BC.** Reasons:

- **Ontario** restricts the controlled act of psychotherapy and the
  "psychotherapist" titles to regulated members
  ([CRPO – Unregulated Practitioners](https://crpo.ca/complaints-reports/unregulated-practitioners/)).
  How this applies to an out-of-province practitioner seeing Ontario
  clients by video isn't settled in what we found.
- **Quebec** reserves psychotherapy and the psychotherapist title for
  permit holders ([Ordre des psychologues du Québec](https://www.ordrepsy.qc.ca/english/what-are-unlawful-practices-usurpation-title)).
- **Nova Scotia** and **PEI** protect the counselling therapist title, and
  New Brunswick's status was unclear in the sources found
  ([CCPA – Nova Scotia](https://www.ccpa-accp.ca/fr/nova-scotia-regulation/);
  [PEI regulation](https://qatauth.gpei.ca/sites/default/files/legislation/r10-1-01-1-regulated_health_professions_act_counselling_therapists_regulations.pdf)).
- **BC** has designated psychotherapy as a health profession under the
  Health Professions and Occupations Act. BCACC says regulation starts
  November 29, 2027
  ([CHCPBC](https://chcpbc.org/2025/07/17/new-health-profession-legislation-comes-into-force-on-april-1-2026);
  [BCACC FAQ](https://bcacc.ca/?p=137750)). Title rules may change, so
  watch this.

This doesn't change who she *can* see. That's the ethics reviewer's call.
It only sets where we spend marketing effort.
